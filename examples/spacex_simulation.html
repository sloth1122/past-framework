PAST Framework — What This Simulation Proves
1. Same stock, opposite behavior. Alpha's Patience=6 kept him out of the IPO euphoria. Beta's Conviction=7 put him in immediately. PAST didn't change the stock — it changed how each agent PERCEIVED the same data.

2. Personality drives entry, rules drive exit. Beta's conviction made him buy at $160. But the 10% stop loss RULE (not personality) forced him out at $144.86. PAST tunes the personality; the skill file defines the rules. This separation is what prevents disaster.

3. Rocky tunes the person, not the strategy. Rocky didn't tell Alpha to stop trading. He increased Patience 6→7 (tighter entry criteria). He didn't tell Beta to abandon his thesis. He increased Impulsivity 5→6 (more scrutiny on entry timing). PAST is contextual reinforcement learning — the agents learn from their PAST without weight updates.

4. Capital preservation beats conviction. Alpha lost $9.78 over 6 weeks. Beta lost $250.24 on one trade. In the Arena, the agent with the better P&L wins — and Alpha's conservative personality (Risk=3, Patience=6) was better suited to a post-IPO downtrend. Beta's bold personality (Risk=6, Conviction=7) would win in a bull market. The Arena's value is in seeing which personality wins in WHICH market.
